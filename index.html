Section 8.17 – 8.18 • Foundation Concept

What is a Gravitational Field ($\vec{E}$)?

Field Intensity Formulas

$$\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m_0} \quad \text{and} \quad E = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

🌐 Concept of Gravitational Field

Newton's law of gravitation tells us that two masses attract each other across empty space without physical contact (action-at-a-distance). To explain how this interaction propagates through space, physics introduces the Field Concept:

Definition: The gravitational field of a material body is the three-dimensional space surrounding it within which its gravitational force of attraction can be experienced by any other body. Theoretically, this field extends up to infinity ($r \to \infty$), becoming progressively weaker with distance.

🎯 Gravitational Field Intensity ($\vec{E}$)

The strength of the gravitational field at any point is measured by Gravitational Field Intensity ($\vec{E}$), defined as the gravitational force experienced by a unit test mass ($m_0 = 1\text{ kg}$) placed at that point: $$\vec{E} = \lim_{m_0 \to 0} \frac{\vec{F}}{m_0} = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{r}$$

🧭 Gravitational Lines of Force

Gravitational fields are visualized using radial inward field lines pointing straight toward the centre of the source mass $M$. The crowding (density) of lines represents field strength—dense near the surface ($E \propto 1/r^2$) and spreading apart in deep space.

📐 NCERT & Reference Properties of $\vec{E}$

Physical Nature Vector (Radially Inward)
SI Unit $\text{N kg}^{-1}$ or $\text{m s}^{-2}$
CGS Unit $\text{dyn g}^{-1}$ or $\text{cm s}^{-2}$
Dimensional Formula (Ref. Pg 8.21):
$$[E] = \frac{[F]}{[m]} = \frac{[MLT^{-2}]}{[M]} = [M^0 L T^{-2}]$$
Equivalence with $g$ $E_{\text{surface}} = \frac{GM}{R^2} = g \approx 9.8\text{ N/kg}$
💡 Key Insight: $\vec{E}$ depends only on the source mass $M$ and distance $r$—never on the test mass $m_0$!

Section 8.19 • Core Concept

What is Gravitational Potential Energy ($U$)?

Key NCERT Formula

$$U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$$

📘 NCERT Definition

When two bodies are placed close to one another, they interact through the gravitational force and possess mutual gravitational potential energy.

"The gravitational potential energy of a body is the energy associated with it due to its position in the gravitational field of another body and is measured by the amount of work done in bringing a body from infinity to a given point in the gravitational field of the other."

🎯 Zero Level of Potential Energy

When one body lies at infinity ($r = \infty$) from another, the gravitational force on it is zero ($F \to 0$). Consequently, its potential energy is chosen as zero ($U = 0$). This reference is called the zero level of potential energy.

➖ Why is $U$ Always Negative?

Gravitational force is purely attractive. As a body approaches Earth from $\infty$, work is done by the gravitational field itself. Mutual energy is expended, so $U$ drops below zero ($U < 0$), indicating a bound system.

🌍 Real-Life Connections

  • 1
    Rockets & Escape Velocity: A rocket on Earth sits deep inside a negative potential well ($U = -GMm/R$). Thrusters must supply positive kinetic energy equal to $+GMm/R$ so total energy reaches $0$ at infinity!
  • 2
    Hydroelectric Power Dams: Water stored at height $h$ has higher gravitational P.E. ($\Delta U \approx mgh$). Falling water converts this $\Delta U$ into kinetic and electrical energy.
  • 3
    Bound Planetary & Satellite Orbits: Planets remain bound to the Sun and satellites orbit Earth because their total mechanical energy ($E = K + U < 0$) stays negative.
SI Unit: Joule (J) Dimensions: $[M^1 L^2 T^{-2}]$

Exam Derivation • Reference Q.33 & Fig. 8.22

Expression for Gravitational Potential Energy ($U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$)

Step-by-step integral derivation of gravitational P.E. of a body of mass $m$ located at distance $r$ from the centre of the Earth ($r > R$).

📐 Fig. 8.22 Interactive Diagram

Move Test Mass $A$

Use the slider below to bring the test mass $m$ from $\infty$ through intermediate point $A$ ($OA = x$) by infinitesimal step $AB = dx$ to target point $P$ ($OP = r$).

Position of Body $A$ ($x$): x = 3.2 R (Approaching P)
At Infinity ($x = \infty$) At Target $P$ ($x = r$)
1

Setup & Instantaneous Gravitational Force

Suppose the Earth is a uniform sphere of mass $M$ and radius $R$. We wish to calculate the potential energy of a body of mass $m$ located at point $P$ such that $OP = r$ ($r > R$). Suppose at any instant the body is at point $A$ at distance $OA = x$. By Newton's Law of Gravitation, the attractive force on the body at $A$ is:

$$F = \frac{GMm}{x^2}$$
2

Small Work Done for Infinitesimal Displacement ($dx$)

Since the force $F$ varies inversely with $x^2$, it cannot be treated as constant over large distances. However, over a very small displacement $AB = dx$ towards Earth, $F$ remains practically constant. The small work done $dW$ is:

$$dW = F \, dx = \frac{GMm}{x^2} \, dx$$
3

Integrating from Infinity ($x = \infty$) to Point $P$ ($x = r$)

The total work done $W$ in bringing the body from infinity ($x = \infty$) to point $P$ ($x = r$) is obtained by integrating $dW$ between limits $x = \infty$ and $x = r$:

$$W = \int dW = \int_{\infty}^{r} \frac{GMm}{x^2} \, dx = GMm \int_{\infty}^{r} x^{-2} \, dx$$
4

Evaluating the Definite Integral

Using the standard power rule $\int x^{-2} \, dx = \frac{x^{-2+1}}{-2+1} = -\frac{1}{x}$ and noting that $\frac{1}{\infty} = 0$:

$$W = GMm \left[ -\frac{1}{x} \right]_{\infty}^{r} = -GMm \left[ \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{\infty} \right] = -\frac{GMm}{r}$$
5

Final NCERT Expression for Gravitational Potential Energy

By definition, this work done is stored as the gravitational potential energy $U$ of the body of mass $m$ at distance $r$ from the centre of the Earth:

$$U = -\frac{GMm}{r} \qquad \cdots (i)$$

📌 Four Important NCERT Deductions (Reference Page 8.22)

Point 1 • Significance of Negative Sign

The negative sign in equation $(i)$ indicates that the potential energy is due to gravitational attraction between the Earth and the body. When the body is brought from infinity to distance $r$, work is done by the gravitational force of attraction. As mutual energy of the two bodies is expended, their energy reduces by this amount.

Point 2 • Variation with Distance $r$

As the distance $r$ increases, the magnitude $\frac{GMm}{r}$ becomes smaller, which means the negative value becomes less negative—so gravitational P.E. increases until it reaches zero (its maximum value) at $r = \infty$.

Point 3 • Change in P.E. Between $r_1$ and $r_2$

If a body of mass $m$ is moved from distance $r_1$ to $r_2$, the change in potential energy is: $$\Delta U = \int_{r_1}^{r_2} \frac{GMm}{x^2} \, dx = GMm \left[ -\frac{1}{x} \right]_{r_1}^{r_2} = GMm \left[ \frac{1}{r_1} - \frac{1}{r_2} \right]$$ If $r_1 > r_2$, then $\Delta U$ is negative (P.E. decreases when brought closer to Earth).

Point 4 • Reduction to $\Delta U = mgh$ near Surface

Moving from surface ($r_1 = R$) to height $h$ ($r_2 = R + h$): $$\Delta U = GMm \left[ \frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{R+h} \right] = \frac{GMm}{R} \left[ 1 - \left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^{-1} \right]$$ Applying Binomial Theorem for $h \ll R$, $(1 + \frac{h}{R})^{-1} \approx 1 - \frac{h}{R}$: $$\Delta U = \frac{GMm}{R}\left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{h}{R}\right)\right] = \frac{GMmh}{R^2} = mgh \quad \left(\because g = \frac{GM}{R^2}\right)$$

Section 8.20 • Reference Q.34

Gravitational Potential ($V$)

Defining Formula

$$V = \frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Mass}} = \frac{W}{m} = -\frac{GM}{r}$$

📘 NCERT Definition of Gravitational Potential

1. Energy Perspective: The gravitational potential at a point is the potential energy associated with a unit mass ($1\text{ kg}$) due to its position in the gravitational field of another body.

2. Work Perspective: The gravitational potential at a point in the gravitational field of a body is defined as the amount of work done in bringing a body of unit mass from infinity to that point.

💡 Why Do Physicists Define $V$ Separately from $U$?

Gravitational Potential Energy ($U = -GMm/r$) depends on both the planet ($M$) and the test object ($m$). By dividing out $m$, Gravitational Potential ($V = -GM/r$) becomes a pure property of the source gravitational field alone! Once you know the potential map $V(r)$ around a planet, you can instantly find the potential energy of any satellite of mass $m$ simply by multiplying $U = mV$.

📊 Physical Quantities, Units & Dimensions

Physical Nature Scalar Quantity
SI Unit $\text{J kg}^{-1}$ (Joule per kg)
CGS Unit $\text{erg g}^{-1}$
Dimensional Formula:
$$[V] = \frac{[W]}{[m]} = \frac{[ML^2T^{-2}]}{[M]} = [M^0 L^2 T^{-2}]$$
Value at Infinity ($r = \infty$) $V_{\infty} = 0$ (Maximum)

Section 8.20 • Reference Q.35 & Connected Relations

Derivations & Inter-Relations of Gravitational Potential

Derivation A (Q.35)

Gravitational Potential at a Point Due to Earth

The work done in bringing a body of mass $m$ from infinity to a point at distance $r$ from the centre of the Earth is: $$W = -\frac{GMm}{r}$$ Hence, the gravitational potential due to the Earth at distance $r$ from its centre is: $$V = \frac{W}{m} = -\frac{GM}{r}$$

At Earth's Surface ($r = R$):

$$V_{\text{surface}} = -\frac{GM}{R} = -gR$$

Derivation B (Q.35)

Relation Between Gravitational P.E. ($U$) & Potential ($V$)

Comparing the expressions for potential energy $U$ and potential $V$ at distance $r$ from Earth's centre: $$U = -\frac{GMm}{r} = \left( -\frac{GM}{r} \right) \times m$$ Therefore, potential energy is the product of mass and gravitational potential:

Fundamental Bridge Equation:

$$U = m \cdot V \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta U = m \cdot \Delta V$$

Derivation C (NCERT)

Relation Between Field Intensity ($E$) & Potential ($V$)

Work done by gravitational force $F = mE$ over small displacement $dr$ is $dW = -m\,dV = mE\,dr$. Differentiating $V = -GM/r$ with respect to $r$: $$\frac{dV}{dr} = \frac{d}{dr}\left(-\frac{GM}{r}\right) = +\frac{GM}{r^2} = -E$$

Potential Gradient Relation:

$$E = -\frac{dV}{dr}$$
